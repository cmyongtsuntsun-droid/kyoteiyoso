How to read AI predictions and place bets

Our predictions use a machine-learning model (LightGBM LambdaRank) to analyze entries, racer records, motor performance, pre-race trial times and weather at all 24 venues. It ranks the six boats in each race relative to one another. A lower value in the Prediction column means the AI ranks that boat higher. AI Win Probability estimates the chance of finishing first.

Checklist for first-time visitors

  • Start with races where the AI's top pick has a win probability above 50%
  • Boat 1 starts from the inside lane, where an inside lead is more common
  • Keep trifecta combinations selective; consider the top two or three suggestions first
  • Strong winds (4 m/s or more) can make races less predictable; consider smaller bets or skipping
  • Racers you're interested inRacer analysisand check the course-by-course results

AI probabilities and suggested trifecta bets

A trifecta requires predicting the first-, second- and third-place finishers in exact order, with 120 possible combinations. Our Plackett–Luce model estimates each combination's probability and lists the strongest picks first. Hit rates are naturally limited, while payouts can be larger.

Boat-racing glossary

National win rate
A racer's average win rate nationwide; a basic measure of performance.
Motor top-two rate
The share of races in which a motor finished in the top two; a measure of motor performance.
Pre-race trial time
Lap time from the pre-race trial run; an indicator of the boat's speed before the start.
ST (start timing)
Start timing. A smaller value means a quicker start; racers with a faster average ST have an advantage.
Inside-lane lead
When the boat in lane 1 leads around the first turn and goes on to win.
3連単
A bet on the first three finishers in exact order. There are 120 combinations, with the chance of a larger payout.
Entry lane
The lane a racer actually enters at the start; it may differ from the assigned boat number.
Motor trim angle
The motor's mounting angle. Raising it favors top speed; lowering it favors acceleration.
